EDA Observations (paper ko Results/Discussion section ko lagi)
1. Onion sabai bhanda volatile crop — std deviation 41.6 (Potato 16.7, Tomato 22.2 bhanda dherai badi), price trend graph ma sabai bhanda thulo spikes (140 samma). Kaaran: India bata import huने भएले supply chain disruption ले dherai affect garcha.
2. Monsoon season ma price maathi + volatile hunчha — boxplot ma sabai crop ko Monsoon box maathi ra outliers dherai. Kaaran: baadi/pahiro le supply disrupt garcha.
3. Data leakage/aggregation issue bhetiyo ra fix garyou — sub-varieties (jastai "Onion Dry" vs "Onion Green") lai group नगरी lag feature banauda correlation galat (Onion=0.058) dekhieko thiyo. Daily aggregation (groupby(['Crop','Date'])) garepachi correlation thik bhayo — yo methodology section ko strong point ho.
4. Price_Lag1 ra Price_Lag7 bic 0.83 correlation — hijo ra hapta agadiko price aapasma similar, matlab price ekदम achानक change hudaina.
5. Data leakage fix — before aggregation, lag correlation was misleadingly 
   low (Onion: 0.058) because sub-varieties (e.g., "Onion Dry" vs "Onion 
   Green") were mixed in the same time series. After grouping by (Crop, 
   Date) to compute one daily average price per crop, lag correlations 
   became strong and realistic:
   - Onion: 0.965
   - Potato: 0.976
   - Tomato: 0.958
   This confirms Price_Lag1 is a powerful predictor for the models.
   6. Model Comparison Results:
   Linear Regression: MAE=3.965, RMSE=7.180, R²=0.9186 (best)
   Ridge Regression:   MAE=3.966, RMSE=7.179, R²=0.9186 (best, tied)
   Decision Tree:      MAE=4.496, RMSE=8.080, R²=0.8969
   SVR:                MAE=4.875, RMSE=9.120, R²=0.8686
   
   Linear and Ridge outperform tree-based and kernel methods, suggesting 
   the price relationship (especially via Price_Lag1) is predominantly 
   linear. This aligns with the strong lag correlations found in EDA 
   (0.95-0.98 per crop).
   7. Feature Scaling Comparison (Comparative Study):
   Linear Regression: R² unchanged (0.9186 → 0.9186) — scale-invariant model
   Ridge Regression:   R² unchanged (0.9186 → 0.9186) — scale-invariant model
   Decision Tree:      R² unchanged (0.8969 → 0.8969) — threshold-based splits, scale-invariant
   SVR:                R² improved slightly (0.8658 → 0.8686) — distance-based model,
                        benefits from scaling but improvement was modest since 
                        Price_Lag1/Lag7 already shared a similar numeric range.
   
   Conclusion: Feature scaling has negligible effect on tree-based and linear 
   models, and only a modest effect on SVR in this dataset — because the 
   dominant features (lag prices) already occupied a comparable scale.
   8. Actual vs Predicted Analysis (Linear Regression, best model):
   Points cluster tightly around the perfect-prediction line, especially 
   in the lower price range (25-100 NPR/Kg). Slight scatter increases at 
   higher prices (150-200 NPR/Kg), corresponding to rare price spikes 
   (mainly from Onion volatility).

9. Residual Analysis:
   Residuals are randomly scattered around zero with no strong systematic 
   pattern, confirming the model has no major bias. A mild increase in 
   residual spread at higher predicted prices (heteroscedasticity) suggests 
   the model is slightly less precise for extreme/rare price values — a 
   known limitation to note in the paper's Discussion/Limitations section.
